# Independent Technical Portfolio

Four small, independent projects, read as the stages of one supply planning system that informs restocking decisions rather than making them: what counts as demand, which inputs can be trusted, what each shipping plan trades away, and whether people use the tool. Each uses fictional data and has a case study you can check against its code.

**Live site:** https://h-yang-26.github.io/

| Stage | Project | What the system does | What a person decides | Links |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Demand signal | B2B Sales Systems Case Study | Keeps quotes, orders and shipments apart and counts committed demand once. | Sales and planning judge what that demand means for restocking. | [Case study](https://h-yang-26.github.io/projects/b2b-sales-systems-case-study/) · [Repo](https://github.com/h-yang-26/b2b-sales-systems-case-study) |
| 2 · Clean inputs | Synthetic Data Quality Pipeline | Fixes only what has one right answer and sends every other row to review, with its reason. | A reviewer makes every ambiguous call. | [Case study](https://h-yang-26.github.io/projects/synthetic-data-quality-pipeline/) · [Repo](https://github.com/h-yang-26/synthetic-data-quality-pipeline) |
| 3 · Trade-offs | Inventory and Shipping Optimization Lab | Compares shipping plans and what each one leaves short. | A planner sets what a shortage costs and picks the plan. | [Case study](https://h-yang-26.github.io/projects/inventory-shipping-optimization-lab/) · [Repo](https://github.com/h-yang-26/inventory-shipping-optimization-lab) |
| 4 · Adoption | Product Usage Analytics Demo | Validates the log first, then writes down how each number is counted. | Admins decide what to look into next. | [Case study](https://h-yang-26.github.io/projects/product-usage-analytics-demo/) · [Repo](https://github.com/h-yang-26/product-usage-analytics-demo) |

**How these were built:** I directed these projects: ChatGPT and Claude helped design and plan them, and Codex and Claude Code did the technical work. [How these were built](https://h-yang-26.github.io/how-these-were-built/) explains who decided what and how the results were checked.

All data in these projects is fictional, and the results say nothing about my employer’s systems.
