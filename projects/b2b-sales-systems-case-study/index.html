Professional work and public-demo boundary

Relevant professional responsibility

I collaborate with a software lead in a two-person team on an industrial B2B platform used by staff and dealers, contributing business context, requirements clarification, reviews and AI-assisted development direction for sales and operational workflows.

Scope of this public demonstration

This public case study proposes a hypothetical sales lifecycle and conceptual module boundaries. It includes a fictional scenario and checking scripts, not the employer's architecture, software, deployed CRM/ERP or integrations.

The problem

A quotation, an accepted order, reserved stock and a physical shipment describe different business facts. This fictional distributor scenario illustrates why combining those quantities can double count demand, and how clear definitions and logical ownership keep the narrative and metrics consistent.

System diagrams and synthetic results

What the demonstration contains

The documentation covers a sales lifecycle, an entity glossary, a Mermaid business-process diagram, a conceptual module diagram, three architecture decision records and five metric definitions. A shared fictional JSON example supports a small arithmetic and consistency calculator and standard-library tests.

The modules and ownership boundaries are a proposed design, not an implemented or deployed CRM/ERP. The executable work consists of the checking scripts; it does not implement the depicted business application.

Technical approach

The shared snapshot is quote 4, order 2, ship 1, remaining 1. The other two quoted units are unconverted offers, not additional committed demand. A stock reservation is an allocation of on-hand units, not another sale or shipment. Estimated dates, observed facts and missing values remain distinct.

With cancellations and returns excluded, remaining quantity is ordered_quantity - shipped_quantity. In the later fictional walkthrough, the second unit ships: shipped becomes two and remaining becomes zero, while committed demand remains two. The conceptual architecture assigns logical owners for these facts without requiring separate services or databases.

Metrics use distinct populations. The win-rate calculator counts distinct opportunities, excludes and reports missing or out-of-period close dates, and rejects conflicting opportunity facts. Two quote rows for one opportunity therefore do not create two wins.

Tradeoffs and limitations

The scenario has one SKU, warehouse, currency and price, with five invented cohort rows. Cancellations, returns, tax, discounts and allocation among competing orders are excluded. Numbers illustrate arithmetic and denominators, not measured business performance.

The optional calculator assumes preselected cohort rows for the other metrics. It does not enforce every documented issue-date, acceptance-date or missing-lineage exclusion rule, so it is not presented as a full reporting system. The document checker covers structure, identifiers and selected statements; it cannot establish complete semantic correctness or readability. Other operating systems and Python versions are not tested.

My contribution

I co-developed the original agent prompt kit for this project with AI assistance, and I provided the business context, project purpose and initial architecture outline. I set requirements, evaluation criteria, edge-case priorities and approval decisions.

I used a planner-and-builder AI workflow to produce the written artifacts, fictional scenario and checking scripts. AI tools produced those materials, while I remained responsible for the project's direction and final approvals. This contribution concerns the hypothetical case study, not an implementation of its proposed business platform.

During the first review round, I personally followed the project's run-and-check instructions in PowerShell and confirmed that the outputs matched the agents' reports. Later corrections and their regression results were verified by agents.

What I learned

I learned to separate quotations, commercial commitments, stock reservations and physical shipments, and to identify which responsibility owns each fact. The scenario also prompted questions about keeping reservation updates consistent when operations happen concurrently. Transactions and concurrency control are relevant implementation topics for a future prototype; this case study remains a conceptual design.
