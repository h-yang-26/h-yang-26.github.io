Before and after: the public model

Professional work and public-demo boundary

Relevant professional responsibility

I designed and directed AI-assisted development of a forklift restock recommendation capability covering demand forecasts, live demand and shipping-container utilization. The capability was integrated into the business platform and continues to be developed.

Scope of this public demonstration

This public integer-programming lab isolates a single-period container allocation tradeoff, with fictional demand and costs. It does not reproduce the business platform's forecasting, replenishment, live integrations or operational decisions.

The problem

Shipping every requested unit may not be worthwhile when opening a container incurs a fixed charge. This fictional laboratory models that tradeoff explicitly: allocate available stock under demand, mass and volume constraints while balancing shortage penalties against container charges.

System diagrams and synthetic results

What the demonstration contains

A Python command-line model reads fictional scenarios, solves integer allocation decisions with PuLP and CBC, and reports shipments, shortages, container use, utilization, solver status and objective cost. Text and JSON exports replace their designated files on rerun. Five bundled scenarios illustrate normal capacity, tighter capacity, higher penalties, low penalties and no inventory.

Technical approach

Allocations and shortages are nonnegative integers; container-use decisions are binary. Shipments cannot exceed stock, and shipped units plus shortage must equal demand. Each used container must satisfy both its mass and approximate-volume limit.

The objective minimizes shortage penalties plus fixed charges, entirely in fictional toy_cost_units. A separate checker revalidates decisions and recomputes their cost before a plan is interpreted. OPTIMAL requires supporting solver status; a valid incumbent without an optimality proof is reported as FEASIBLE.

The README's hand-checkable example has demand for five units, each weighing 10 kg and occupying 1 m³. A 25 kg / 3 m³ container permits two units. With penalty 10 and fixed charge 12, shipping two costs 3 × 10 + 12 = 42, compared with 50 for shipping nothing.

Tradeoffs and limitations

Aggregate mass and volume feasibility is not a validated 3D packing, stacking or load-restraint plan. Holding costs, forecasting and multi-period planning are excluded. Low penalties can make shipping nothing optimal; zero stock produces shortages rather than infeasibility. Utilization uses used-container capacity and reports N/A for a zero denominator.

The solver runs in CBC's native serial mode (threads=0), which resolved a reproduced hang on the tested binary. A universal wall-clock runtime bound is not verified; solver limits are not operating-system deadlines. PuLP is pinned and emits deprecation warnings. All costs and baseline differences are fictional, not real savings.

My contribution

I co-developed the original agent prompt kit for this project with AI assistance, and I provided the business context, project purpose and initial architecture outline. I set requirements, evaluation criteria, edge-case priorities and approval decisions.

I used a planner-and-builder AI workflow to produce the implementation and supporting documentation. AI tools performed the implementation work, while I remained responsible for the project's direction and final approvals.

During the first review round, I personally followed the project's run-and-check instructions in PowerShell and confirmed that the outputs matched the agents' reports. Later corrections and their regression results were verified by agents.

What I learned

I learned how product dimensions and container capacities change which allocations are feasible, and how shortage penalties influence whether opening another container is worthwhile. The exercise helped me distinguish a shipment-allocation decision from longer-term inventory strategy. Broader forecasting, inventory-policy and convex/nonconvex model-selection questions remain areas for further study.

Source and reproducibility

Source on GitHub: Inventory and Shipping Optimization LabREADME and run instructions

The README documents dependency setup and the python -m shiplab entry point.

Windows PowerShell, from the repository root:

py -3.11 -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\python -m pip install -r requirements-dev.txt
.\.venv\Scripts\python -m shiplab
.\.venv\Scripts\python -m pytest

Tested environment: Windows 11, Python 3.13.15, PuLP 3.3.2, CBC 2.10.3 (bundled with PuLP), pytest 9.1.1 (agent-run).

The Python projects run locally from their repositories. This static site shows their recorded results; it does not run them.

Independence statement: This is a fictional independent optimization demonstration. Its outputs illustrate the implemented model and do not establish production performance, freight economics or employer outcomes.
